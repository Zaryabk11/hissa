### Behind the design

We started with a simple observation: at a family table, you may not choose what’s cooked—but you can choose your portion. So instead of making Hissa feel like another calorie-focused diet app, we grounded it in familiar measures like roti and katori, with estimates stated plainly and no food shaming.

The design moved from an earlier dark, robot-led direction to a warmer, more human one: saffron and green, layered Urdu lettering, and painted dishes. We avoided decorative cultural patterns; the food and people at the table should carry the cultural detail. The page then tells a story—from a plate, to the idea of “your share,” to friends, to how Hissa works—using scroll transitions to connect each scene. The aim is to make Hissa feel familiar, practical, honest, and kind.

Hi, we’re building Hissa for people who eat the food already on their family’s table. In many desi households, you don’t choose what gets cooked—you choose how much to take. Hissa helps make that choice easier, using familiar measures like roti and katori, with numbers clearly labelled as estimates.

We wanted the landing page to feel made for that audience: warm, recognisable and social—not like a generic calorie app. We drew inspiration from Pakistani truck art, Supersolid’s fluid wordmark and smooth scrolling, and the bold layouts of Mammoth Murals, balanced with Monolog’s simpler navigation.

As we explored, we realised that adding patterns like jaali and chain borders felt like trying too hard. So we let the food and people carry the cultural character instead. The saffron-and-green palette, Urdu-first wordmark and painted dishes make the page feel expressive and rooted, while clear copy keeps the product easy to understand.

The page unfolds as a story: first the plate, then the idea of “your hissa,” then the friends-and-family community, followed by how the product works, its features, familiar dishes and a final invitation to try Hissa. Scroll transitions connect those moments; subtle WebGL effects bring the artwork and wordmark to life. The playful liquid navbar adds a little personality without getting in the way.

Above all, we wanted someone to land on the page and think: *this understands the food I eat, it won’t judge me, and I can make progress with my people.*
